Executive summary

Reading App

An offline iPad app that teaches one kindergarten reader to sound words out instead of guessing, and builds her confidence one small, successful step at a time.

Version 1.0.0 · Built 17 November 2026 from READING_APP_SPEC.md v1.0 · Phases 0–5 complete · Pilot (Phase 6) not started

Bottom line

The app is built and tested, and is ready to deploy and install. It is not ready for her to use until you record about 30 letter sounds on the iPad (10 to 15 minutes in the built-in Sound Studio). Everything else works now.

The problem it addresses

  • She knows many words, but a block of text overwhelms her.
  • At an unfamiliar word she guesses or gives up.
  • The working theory: the cause is text load and a missing decoding routine, not vocabulary.

How it works

  • One routine, everywhere: tap each sound, slide to blend, say the word. It is the only help the app ever offers, and the whole word cannot be heard until she has done it.
  • Only readable text: every word is checked against what her class has taught. Anything else is refused by the code, not by convention.
  • Text grows gradually: one phrase at a time now, moving to full pages as her accuracy holds at 90%.
  • No pressure: no timers, scores, lives, buzzers or lost streaks. She never ends on a miss.

Content at launch

  • 279 words, each split into sounds by rule and checked
  • 19 heart words from her list, plus 4 optional interest words
  • 103 sentences and 23 short stories
  • Levels 1–3 unlocked (short a, i, o; ch, th), matching her class today

Built around what she loves

  • Animals run through nearly every story: cats, dogs, pigs, a hog in the fog.
  • Space with words she can already read: a bot in a pod, a hot dot, a ship.
  • Princesses and fairies as optional heart words (star, moon, fairy, princess) with five stories, off until you switch them on.
  • Reward stickers: rocket, crown, wand, unicorn, planet, paw.

What it needs from you

  1. Deploy it (about 10 minutes). GitHub Pages or Netlify; both are documented and need no server.
  2. Install it on her iPad from Safari with Add to Home Screen, then open it once while online.
  3. Record the sounds in Sound Studio. About 30 single sounds first. Words and spoken directions can follow, or use the iPad's voice for those in the meantime.
  4. Read through the content in content/REVIEW.md. It was drafted for this app and machine-checked, but you should confirm it.
  5. Run the device checklist once (15 minutes), then start the pilot.

Decisions I made that you may want to reverse

DecisionWhy
In read-then-match, the pictures wait for her tap. They never appear on a 4-second timer.A timer would show pictures before a slow reader finishes, which breaks the spec's own rule that pictures come only after reading.
The iPad's voice can stand in for unrecorded words and directions, as a parent setting (off by default).There are 302 word clips. Without this the app would be largely silent until all are recorded. It is never used for single letter sounds.
Four interest words (star, moon, fairy, princess) ship switched off.Her interests are not decodable yet. Heart words are the honest way to include them, and that is your call.
Book spines on her shelf show a picture, not a title.Small text would break the minimum reading sizes. The text appears full-size when she opens the book.
An extra anti-guessing rule: an answer needs one second of no tapping first.Testing showed that mashing the whole screen could occasionally land an answer.

Privacy

All data stays on the iPad. The app makes no network requests while running, and has no accounts, analytics or third-party code. Her progress leaves the device only if you export a backup file.
